\title{A Faster Tableau for TCTL}
\author{}
\institute{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A zone-based tableau for TCTL satisfiability is exponential in the size of the
formula, and in practice the exponent has two quite different sources. We
separate them by measurement: on the family that costs the most, the symbolic
part of a node contributes nothing at all --- there is exactly one zone per
distinct discrete state --- and the quarter of a million nodes are a quarter of
a million combinations of which obligations are still outstanding. No
abstraction, no widening and no inclusion test can reduce that number, because
there is nothing there to abstract.

We show that a large part of it is an artefact of representation. When a node's
goals fall into groups that mention disjoint symbols, those groups have disjoint
formula-clocks, and the node is realisable exactly when each group is; deciding
them separately replaces a product of state spaces by a sum. The theorem needs
no condition on the zone, which we had expected it to. Applied at every node
with a cache, and at the root as a preprocessing step, it turns $2^{k}$ into
$O(k)$ on conjunctions of independent obligations: at $k=20$, $4\,195\,583$
nodes and $31.4$ seconds become $240$ nodes and under ten milliseconds.

We report what the same measurements rule out. Conflict-driven search, the
obvious import from SAT, has nothing to work on: on the hardest instance every
node is accepted and none is closed. Zone inclusion cannot fire, because the
several zones a discrete state carries are disjoint cells rather than nested
ones. We also record six further attempts that failed, with the reason each
failed, and a soundness bug the evaluation harness found in the process.
\end{abstract}
