============================================================
Benchmark Renaming Rationale
TCTL Tableau Solver — CAV Evaluation
============================================================


NAMING POLICY
------------------------------------------------------------

Internal benchmark identifiers such as "A2_overlap_sat", "B6_forced_delay_
noextr", or "H2_chain_killed" are optimised for programmer readability
during development: they are terse, use abbreviations, and embed
implementation-level detail (e.g. "noextr" for "no extrapolation") that
is opaque to a reader unfamiliar with the codebase.  For a publication
targeting a formal-methods venue (CAV, TACAS), benchmark names appear in
figure legends, table headers, captions, and running-text discussion.
They must therefore be self-explanatory, scientifically precise, and
consistent across families.

The revised names follow four principles: (1) expose the *structural
phenomenon* being tested rather than restating the raw code identifier;
(2) preserve the SAT/UNSAT distinction when it is a defining property of
the benchmark; (3) use standard temporal-logic terminology (until,
release, fixpoint, eventuality, safety, liveness); and (4) maintain
uniform capitalisation and phrasing across all families so that figures
and tables read fluently.  Short labels (2–4 words) are provided for
legend text where space is constrained; full names are used in captions,
appendix tables, and discussion paragraphs.


FAMILY-BY-FAMILY RATIONALE
============================================================


A — Depth Scaling
------------------------------------------------------------

This family measures how solver cost grows as the structural depth (or
breadth) of the formula increases.  All four variants share the same
parameterisation axis (depth d) but differ in modality mix, satisfiability
status, and presence of clocks, providing a controlled comparison of
timed versus untimed structural scaling.

  Old:   A1_untimed
  New:   Untimed Depth Baseline
  Short: Untimed Baseline
  Why:   The benchmark alternates AG/EF without any timed operators,
         serving as a clock-free control.  "Baseline" conveys its role
         as a reference point for the timed variants in the same family.

  Old:   A2_overlap_sat
  New:   Overlapping Timed Obligations (SAT)
  Short: Overlap SAT
  Why:   The formula is a conjunction of EF[i, i+M] obligations whose
         windows overlap, forcing zone interaction.  The satisfiable
         verdict is definitional: each obligation can be independently
         scheduled.

  Old:   A3_nested_eu
  New:   Nested Bounded Existential Until
  Short: Nested EU
  Why:   Each nesting level wraps E(true U[1,M] ...) around fresh atoms,
         creating simultaneous clock and eventuality obligations.
         "Bounded Existential Until" is the standard temporal-logic
         term for this operator.

  Old:   A4_killed_unsat
  New:   Unsatisfiable Killed Obligations
  Short: Killed UNSAT
  Why:   The formula adds a universal AG constraint that negates every
         target atom, "killing" all EF obligations.  "Killed" is concise
         and widely understood in the formal-methods community as
         denoting a structurally blocked property.


B — Max-Constant Sensitivity
------------------------------------------------------------

This family isolates the effect of the numeric constant M (max constant
in timing intervals) on solver performance.  Formula structure remains
fixed across M values; only interval endpoints grow, tightening zone
abstraction and forcing finer zone distinctions.

  Old:   B1_EU_delay
  New:   Bounded Until with Mandatory Delay
  Short: EU Delay
  Why:   E(¬p₀ U[0,M] p₀) requires time to advance before satisfaction,
         testing symbolic delay operations.

  Old:   B2_deadline_unsat
  New:   Deadline Conflict (UNSAT)
  Short: Deadline UNSAT
  Why:   The AG[0,M] ¬p₀ globally prevents p₀ within the same window
         where EF[1,M] p₀ demands it.  "Deadline conflict" captures
         the structural contradiction.

  Old:   B3_negated_window
  New:   Negated Timed Modality
  Short: Negated Window
  Why:   EF[0,M] p₀ ∧ ¬EF[0,M] p₀ is a propositional-level timed
         contradiction, testing normalisation of negated bounded
         modalities.

  Old:   B4_chain_windows
  New:   Sequential Window Chain
  Short: Window Chain
  Why:   k=5 EF windows partition [0,M] into consecutive segments.
         Only the endpoints grow with M, isolating numeric-constant
         sensitivity from structural effects.

  Old:   B5_nested_chain
  New:   Nested Bounded Chain
  Short: Nested Chain
  Why:   6-deep nested E(true U[0,M] ...) keeps structure fixed while
         M raises max_constants_ per clock, stressing the extrapolation
         mechanism.

  Old:   B6_forced_delay
  New:   Forced Delay with Extrapolation
  Short: Forced Delay
  Why:   EF[M−1, M] p₀ forces symbolic advance to M−1; with
         extrapolation ON, M-abstraction bounds zone count.

  Old:   B6_forced_delay_noextr
  New:   Forced Delay without Extrapolation
  Short: No Extrapolation
  Why:   The ablation variant disables zone extrapolation, demonstrating
         unbounded zone growth and the necessity of M-abstraction.


C — Concurrent Timed Obligations
------------------------------------------------------------

This family grows the number k of simultaneous timed obligations while
keeping interval constants small, isolating clock-pressure effects from
max-constant sensitivity.

  Old:   C1_staggered
  New:   Staggered Window Obligations
  Short: Staggered
  Why:   Each EF[i, i+2] uses a distinct interval, preventing zone
         overlap.  "Staggered" describes the non-overlapping timing
         pattern.

  Old:   C2_overlapping
  New:   Identical Window Obligations
  Short: Identical Windows
  Why:   All obligations share EF[1,3], maximising zone overlap
         potential.  "Identical" is more precise than "overlapping"
         (which could imply partial overlap).


D — Until Nesting
------------------------------------------------------------

This family increases the nesting depth of existential bounded-until
operators, measuring eventuality-accumulation cost at fixed M.

  Old:   until_nested
  New:   Nested Timed Until
  Short: Nested Until
  Why:   Direct expansion of the abbreviation, using standard
         temporal-logic terminology.

  Old:   until_conjunctive
  New:   Conjunctive Existential Until
  Short: Conjunctive Until
  Why:   This variant grows formula width (independent EU conjuncts)
         rather than depth, providing a dual comparison axis.


E — Release Nesting
------------------------------------------------------------

This family mirrors D but uses release (greatest fixpoint) operators,
testing continuous-preservation obligations at increasing depth.

  Old:   release_nested
  New:   Nested Timed Release
  Short: Nested Release
  Why:   Direct dual of "Nested Timed Until" using the release
         operator, maintaining terminological consistency.


DE — Mixed Until–Release
------------------------------------------------------------

This family conjoins nested-until and nested-release subformulas,
forcing simultaneous least- and greatest-fixpoint resolution.

  Old:   mixed
  New:   Combined Until and Release
  Short: Until + Release
  Why:   The formula is a conjunction of both fixpoint types,
         creating the worst-case interaction for tableau loop
         acceptance.


F — Zeno Rejection
------------------------------------------------------------

These benchmarks are satisfiable under Zeno semantics but correctly
unsatisfiable under time-divergent acceptance.  They validate the
anti-Zeno loop-checking mechanism.

  Old:   F1_ag_false
  New:   Timed Safety Vacuity
  Short: Safety Vacuity
  Why:   AG[1,M] false is vacuously true only if time never enters
         [1,M].  "Safety vacuity" describes the phenomenon: the
         safety property holds vacuously in Zeno runs.

  Old:   F2_eg_conflict
  New:   Zeno Path Conflict
  Short: EG Conflict
  Why:   EG p₀ requires an infinite p₀ path; AG[1,M] ¬p₀ prohibits
         p₀ within the timed window.  The conflict is detectable only
         by rejecting Zeno loops.

  Old:   F3_stacked_zeno
  New:   Stacked Zeno Obligation Traps
  Short: Stacked Traps
  Why:   As M grows, k=M−1 AG/EG pairs each create an independent
         Zeno trap, scaling both zone dimension and the number of
         contradictions the anti-Zeno check must handle.


G — Clock Dimensionality
------------------------------------------------------------

This family grows the number of simultaneously active clocks k while
keeping M small, measuring the quadratic zone-operation cost
(DBM dimension k+1).

  Old:   G1_conjunctive
  New:   Conjunctive Clock Scaling (SAT)
  Short: Conjunctive SAT
  Why:   k staggered EF obligations force k concurrent clocks; the
         satisfiable result confirms zone operations succeed.

  Old:   G2_nested
  New:   Nested Clock Scaling
  Short: Nested Clocks
  Why:   k-deep nested EU prevents early clock deallocation, keeping
         all k clocks simultaneously active.

  Old:   G3_killed
  New:   Conjunctive Clock Scaling (UNSAT)
  Short: Conjunctive UNSAT
  Why:   Adding an AG killer renders G1 unsatisfiable, forcing
         exhaustive search at each clock dimension.


H — Eventuality Chain Length
------------------------------------------------------------

This family isolates formula length (number of chained EU operators)
from max-constant effects by holding M=2 fixed.

  Old:   H1_chain_eu
  New:   Existential Eventuality Chain (SAT)
  Short: Chain SAT
  Why:   n-deep E(pᵢ U[0,2] ...) chain where all growth comes from
         chain length.

  Old:   H2_chain_killed
  New:   Blocked Eventuality Chain (UNSAT)
  Short: Chain UNSAT
  Why:   AG ¬q blocks the terminal eventuality, forcing exhaustive
         search across the full chain.


I — Fixpoint Alternation
------------------------------------------------------------

This family compares formulas of identical structure depth but different
modality composition, all with fixed M=1 and universal quantification.

  Old:   I1_pure_safety
  New:   Pure Safety Nesting (Release)
  Short: Pure Safety
  Why:   n nested AR: only greatest-fixpoint (safety) obligations,
         no eventualities to track.

  Old:   I2_pure_liveness
  New:   Pure Liveness Nesting (Until)
  Short: Pure Liveness
  Why:   n nested AU: every level creates an eventuality, measuring
         pure least-fixpoint cost.

  Old:   I3_alternating
  New:   Alternating Fixpoint Nesting
  Short: Alternating
  Why:   Interleaved AU/AR creates the worst-case for loop acceptance,
         as eventualities compete with safety constraints at every
         other level.
