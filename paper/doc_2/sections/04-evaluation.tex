\section{What it buys}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Selecting the search}

The two searches are a command-line flag, \code{--version v1|v2}, so a
comparison needs no rebuild:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\code{v1} & the search before this work: every optional optimisation off \\
\code{v2} & adds both decomposition forms and goal subsumption \emph{(default)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two things about \code{v1} are worth stating plainly. It is a slower search, not
a wrong one: the onset split points that the shifts introduce are a
\emph{soundness} fix, not an optimisation, and they are in both versions ---
without them $\mathrm{AG}(\mathrm{EF}_{[3,4]}p)\wedge\mathrm{AG}_{[4,5]}\neg p$
is reported satisfiable. And \code{v2} stands down when \code{--model} is
given, because a witness for a decomposed node is the product of its
components' automata and the extractor does not build products.

Measured end to end through the flag:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
case & \code{v1} nodes & \code{v1} time & \code{v2} nodes & \code{v2} time & ratio \\
\midrule
\code{wide} $k{=}16$   & $262\,975$   & $1.35$\,s  & $192$   & $0.00$\,s & $349\times$ \\
\code{wide} $k{=}20$   & $4\,195\,583$& $31.38$\,s & $240$   & $0.00$\,s & $\mathbf{8309\times}$ \\
\code{buried} $k{=}14$ & $66\,181$    & $0.34$\,s  & $731$   & $0.01$\,s & $43\times$ \\
tower $d{=}6$          & $4\,890$     & $0.01$\,s  & $4\,470$& $0.01$\,s & $1\times$ \\
tower $d{=}8$          & $21\,025$    & $0.05$\,s  & $19\,349$&$0.05$\,s & $1\times$ \\
$\mathrm{AG}(\mathrm{EF}p)\wedge\mathrm{AG}(\mathrm{EF}q)$
                       & $352$        & $0.00$\,s  & $92$    & $0.00$\,s & $2\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Run over the whole $5000$-formula suite under both versions: \textbf{zero}
disagreements, and zero mismatches against the by-construction expectations.
The self-test passes $861/861$ under \code{v1}, under \code{v2}, and under every
individual flag combination.

\subsection{The family it was built for}

$\bigwedge_{j<k}\mathrm{EF}_{[2+j,\,4+j]}\,p_j$, per-node decomposition:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$k$ & nodes off & nodes on & time off & time on \\
\midrule
$8$  & $1\,247$      & $271$   & $0.01$\,s & $0.01$\,s \\
$12$ & $16\,863$     & $551$   & $0.08$\,s & $0.01$\,s \\
$14$ & $66\,179$     & $727$   & $0.37$\,s & $0.01$\,s \\
$16$ & $262\,975$    & $941$   & $1.94$\,s & $0.01$\,s \\
$18$ & $1\,049\,619$ & $1\,249$& $9.35$\,s & $0.01$\,s \\
$20$ & $4\,195\,583$ & $1\,869$& $46.66$\,s & $0.01$\,s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The exponential is gone. Node counts $271, 551, 727, 941, 1249, 1869$ are linear
in $k$ against a baseline that doubles, so the ratio is not a constant and grows
without bound: $13\times$ at $k=12$, $244\times$ at $k=16$, $3478\times$ at
$k=20$. Quoting a single speed-up figure would misrepresent it --- the change is
$O(2^k)\to O(k)$, and any number quoted is a statement about which $k$ was
measured.

The static form is stronger still on this shape, having no per-node cost:
$1\,247\to 96$, $16\,863\to 144$, $262\,975\to 192$, and at $k=20$
$4\,195\,583\to 240$ --- $46.66$\,s to below the timer, $11\,884\times$.

\subsection{The two forms compose}

They see different things. The static form splits the root conjunction once and
pays nothing per node; the dynamic form sees independence that only appears
during the search. Neither subsumes the other, and running both takes the better
of the two on every shape measured. Nodes, and time:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
 & plain & static & dynamic & both \\
\midrule
\code{wide} $k{=}16$   & $262\,975$ / $1.91$\,s & $192$ / $0.00$\,s & $941$ / $0.01$\,s & $\mathbf{192}$ \\
\code{wide} $k{=}20$   & $4\,195\,583$ / $46.6$\,s & $240$ / $0.00$\,s & $1\,869$ / $0.01$\,s & $\mathbf{240}$ \\
\code{buried} $k{=}10$ & $4\,437$ / $0.02$\,s & $4\,437$ / $0.02$\,s & $403$ / $0.01$\,s & $\mathbf{403}$ \\
\code{buried} $k{=}14$ & $66\,181$ / $0.45$\,s & $66\,181$ / $0.40$\,s & $731$ / $0.01$\,s & $\mathbf{731}$ \\
\code{mixed} $k{=}14$  & $132\,774$ / $0.76$\,s & $176$ / $0.00$\,s & $750$ / $0.01$\,s & $\mathbf{176}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\code{buried} is $\mathrm{EF}_{[0,1]}(\bigwedge_j \mathrm{EF}_{[a_j,b_j]}p_j)$:
the independence is real but sits under a modality, so the root is not a
conjunction and the static form cannot see it at all --- it returns the
undecomposed node count exactly. \code{mixed} adds one shared conjunct, which
the static split isolates and the dynamic one has to carry through every node.

\noindent All figures in milliseconds. The medians across every family sit near

The honest reading: a large win on the one family that decomposes, and a
$5$--$10\%$ tax everywhere else, paid for running a test that answers ``no''.
The bitmasks and the no-split memo took that tax down from about $30\%$; what
remains is building the item vector at nodes whose shape has not been seen
before.

\subsection{The adversarial suite}

A second suite exists to break \code{v2} rather than to flatter it: ten families
of $220$ instances each, one symbol away from decomposing (\code{almost},
\code{chain}), chained so the transitive closure is one component, buried under
a nest, decomposing recursively, and pushing nesting depth, constant magnitude,
punctual intervals and conjunction width up to $80$. Both versions run on every
instance, with \code{v1} on a shorter budget: it is \code{v2} being measured,
and \code{v1} exhausting its budget is a result rather than something to wait
out.

decomposition never fires cost between two and eight per cent, which is the
honest price of asking.

\subsection{What these numbers do and do not show}

They \emph{do} show that the $2^k$ in \cref{sec:problem} is an artefact of
representation and that removing it is sound on every instance measured
($5000$ formulas, plus a $700$-case soundness sweep over
$\mathrm{AG}(\mathrm{EF}_{[a,b]}p)\wedge\mathrm{AG}_{[c,d]}\neg p$, plus the
$861$-check self-test).

They \emph{do not} show that this helps on formulas people write. \code{wide} is
a family from our own suite, chosen to be a conjunction of independent
obligations, which is exactly the shape the technique targets. The fair claim is
about the mechanism, not about a speed-up on a workload: conjunctions of
independent properties are common in practice --- a safety property and a
liveness property over different signals --- but this suite is not evidence for
how common.

A second caveat: the static form's numbers are on top-level conjunctions, and
splitting a top-level conjunction is close to running the tool twice. It should
be presented as preprocessing, not as a search technique.
