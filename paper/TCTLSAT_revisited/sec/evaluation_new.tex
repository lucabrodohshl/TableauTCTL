\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

We evaluate our procedure through three research questions (RQs):

\begin{enumerate}[start=1,label={\bfseries RQ\arabic*:}, itemindent=2em, ref=\bfseries RQ\arabic*, itemsep=0.1em]
\item \label{rq:structural} Which structural parameters of the input formula drive the procedure's
time and memory, and how does it scale in each?
\item \label{rq:practicality} Is the procedure practical for \emph{specification sanity-checking} ---
deciding the satisfiability of the flat, one-level TCTL queries engineers write for \textsc{Uppaal}?
\item \label{rq:completeness} Completeness is guaranteed only over the non-punctual fragment. What
does the procedure actually leave out, and when?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
\ref{rq:structural} characterises the \emph{worst-case} profile, isolating the parameters that drive
the search and its \textsc{ExpTime} regime; \ref{rq:practicality} asks the complementary
\emph{typical-case} question over the formulas engineers write; and \ref{rq:completeness} asks what
the one gap in the guarantees costs in practice. Table~\ref{tab:bench-overview} is the benchmark in
one view: every family, the parameter it sweeps, and what it is there to establish.

Every instance carries an expected verdict established independently of the tool, so the harness
classifies each disagreement automatically. Because the procedure is sound (Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}),
only two kinds exist: an expected-SAT instance answered \UNSAT{} is an \emph{incompleteness}
instance, and an expected-UNSAT instance answered \SAT{} would be a soundness violation. We report
both; no soundness violation occurred anywhere in this study.

We record wall-clock time (median of three runs), peak resident memory, and the number of tableau
\emph{nodes} explored --- deterministic and machine-independent, and therefore the primary scaling
evidence, with wall-clock reported alongside. Each instance is given a $60$\,s budget,
single-threaded on an Apple~M4~Pro.

\subsection{RQ3: what the procedure leaves out}\label{ss:rq3}

Completeness is guaranteed over the non-punctual fragment and, at nesting depth one, even with
punctual intervals (Corollary~\ref{cor:depthone}). Since the procedure is sound, the one observable
form of incompleteness is an \UNSAT{} verdict on a formula that \emph{is} satisfiable --- and
detecting it needs a notion of satisfiability independent of the tool.

\paragraph{An independent oracle.}
We fix a concrete witness structure and evaluate TCTL on it exactly. The structure $W$ has one
location with a self-loop, no invariant, diverging time, and each atom true on a set of times
periodic with period $P$. Because the location and the labelling never change, every run from a
configuration carries the same atom trace and that trace is a function of time alone; the path
quantifiers therefore collapse on $W$, and TCTL over it becomes a metric logic over the timeline.
Truth sets are then finite unions of rational intervals, and every operator is exact interval
arithmetic: an interval $(l,h)$ of $T(\phi)$ contributes $(l-b,\,h-a)$ to
$T(\mathrm{EF}_{[a,b]}\phi)$, $\mathrm{AG}$ is the dual, and the unbounded cases are settled by
periodicity. A second structure adds a location behind a nondeterministic switch, so that $E$ and
$A$ genuinely differ ($\mathrm{EF}$ unions over the two branches, $\mathrm{AG}$ intersects) --- without
it the universal quantifier is never exercised at all.

If $W$ satisfies $\varphi$ then $\varphi$ is satisfiable, with no appeal to the procedure. We
therefore generate random shapes, keep only those a witness provably satisfies, and ask the solver;
any \UNSAT{} answer is an incompleteness instance. The sweep is concentrated where the theory
implicates a gap: instances the all-true structure already satisfies are discarded (their
obligations are discharged the instant they are raised, so the punctual machinery never runs), and
only formulas carrying a punctual window at modal depth $\ge2$ are kept.

\paragraph{Result.}
Across the sweeps of Table~\ref{tab:rq3-search} the procedure missed no model. The measurement is
not vacuous: pointed at a deliberately weakened acceptance test --- one deciding each loop locally
rather than by the reachability queries of \S\ref{ss:loop} --- the identical pipeline reports a
$3.7\%$ incompleteness rate, every instance carrying a punctual window, at depths two to four. The
search therefore detects the phenomenon it is looking for.

\paragraph{What this does not show.}
The oracle certifies satisfiability by \emph{exhibiting} a model, and every model it can exhibit is
finitely representable. The punctuality caveat concerns the opposite case: formulas whose only
models are \emph{not} finitely representable, where the obligations carried at a given moment depend
on the exact offsets of unboundedly many pending onsets (Fig.~\ref{fig:punctual}). No oracle of this
kind can produce such a formula, so this study cannot exhibit that gap however long it runs.
What it establishes is that within the class of models the procedure is designed to find, none is
missed. Closing the question needs a construction rather than a search.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
The benchmark is synthetic: no standard TCTL-\emph{satisfiability} suite exists, ours being the first
decision procedure for the fragment, so the study is a characterisation and not a comparison. The
\ref{rq:practicality} family mitigates this over the canonical query \emph{shapes}, though on
abstract rather than deployed-model propositions. Located walls are relative to the $60$\,s budget,
and we lean on the deterministic node counts rather than wall-clock, whose absolute values are only
conservative. The \ref{rq:completeness} oracle's limits are stated above. The generator, solver and
a reproducibility manifest accompany the paper.
