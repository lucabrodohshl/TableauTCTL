\section{Two fixes the benchmark forced}
\label{sec:engineering}

Neither belongs to the decomposition. Both were found by an evaluation harness
that carries expected verdicts, and we report them because the first is a
soundness bug in the calculus as published and the second is worth a factor of
seven on its own.

\paragraph{Onsets between split points.} Under a persistent context a modality
is demanded at a continuum of onsets, and the tableau represents them by cells
of the zone. The delay rule split a clock at the endpoints of its own window
only. Under a ban on $\psi$ over $[c,d]$, an onset at $t$ demands $\psi$
somewhere in $[t+a,t+b]$, and that demand is unsatisfiable exactly for
$t\in[c-a,\,d-b]$; splitting the context clock at $c$ and $d$ alone leaves that
interval inside a cell that also holds satisfiable onsets, and the cell's launch
succeeds on one of those. On $\AG(\EF_{[3,4]}p)\wedge\AG_{[4,5]}\neg p$ the bad
onset is the single point $t=1$, no cell isolates it, and the formula --- which
is unsatisfiable --- was reported satisfiable.

A clock must therefore also split at its own endpoints shifted by those of every
\emph{other} modality. The relation is co-liveness, not nesting: in the formula
above the ban and the demand are conjuncts, and a version restricted to
syntactic nesting left every one of the $39$ wrong verdicts in a $700$-case
sweep in place. There are two shifts per pair of modalities and none depends on
the magnitude of a constant, so the count of split points stays quadratic in the
formula and flat in $M$: node counts are $281$, $281$, $281$, $278$ for
$M=4,64,1024,65536$.

\paragraph{A quartic step and two redundant closures.} Profiling put $93\%$ of
the run in the construction of a node's key. Eliminating the clocks a node no
longer reads freed them one at a time, and each free re-closed the matrix, so
the step was $O(K^{4})$. Batching the frees makes it cubic --- freeing only
relaxes, and a closed matrix already carries the tightened value of $d[i][0]$,
so the column each free writes can be read off the original matrix, with the one
correction that a freed clock has lost its own upper bound.

The closure then turns out to be unnecessary altogether. Checking
$d[i][j]\le d[i][l]+d[l][j]$ on the updated matrix given it held on the old one:
a path through a freed clock is $d[i][0]+\infty=\infty$; a freed row is $\infty$
throughout; a freed column becomes $d[i][0]$, and old closure already gave
$d[i][0]\le d[i][l]+d[l][0]$; and the reference row needs
$d[0][l]+d[l][0]\ge 0$, which holds because a closed non-empty zone has no
negative cycle. Max-constant extrapolation gets the same treatment: a zone none
of whose bounds exceeds its clock's maximum is unchanged and arrived closed.

A third defect in the same layer was a correctness one. The minimal-graph packer
writes whole records but not whole words, so with $16$-bit bounds and an odd
constraint count the final half-word was never written, and it sits inside the
length the packer reports. The buffer being reused, the same zone encoded
differently depending on what had been packed before it, and the search rebuilt
the same node under several keys. Clearing the buffer makes the encoding a
function of the zone, and removes $15\%$ of the nodes on a nested tower. The
symptom that exposed it is worth recording: node counts moved when an unrelated
cache size changed, which is not something a correct encoding can do.

Together these take a $17$-clock instance from $12.45$\,s to $1.41$\,s with
identical verdicts.
